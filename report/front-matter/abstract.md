# Abstract

Cloud platforms let enterprises create infrastructure in minutes, but every resource comes with security settings that remain the customer's responsibility: who can read a storage bucket, which addresses can reach a database, what an identity is allowed to do and whether activity is logged. A single wrong value can expose millions of records, and misconfiguration has become one of the most common causes of cloud data breaches. This project studies which misconfigurations create the greatest risk for enterprises and how they can be prevented and detected systematically.

The work builds a taxonomy of twelve misconfiguration classes, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK techniques and NIST SP 800-53 controls, and tests it against ten publicly documented incidents from 2017 to 2024, including Capital One, Toyota, Pegasus Airlines, Microsoft's BlueBleed, Snowflake customer accounts and an Indian case involving SBI Quick. The analysis shows that storage exposure and identity weaknesses dominate, that exposures often remain unnoticed for years, and that the largest breach was a chain of individually moderate weaknesses. A risk assessment of a reference Indian enterprise rates public storage, leaked long-lived keys and missing MFA as critical.

The report proposes a five-stage framework (govern, guardrail, shift left, monitor and respond) and evaluates its central ideas with CloudGuard, a policy-as-code scanner for AWS and Azure Terraform with 39 rules, attack-path analysis, drift detection and SARIF output. CloudGuard reported all 40 issues planted in a vulnerable example estate and none in its hardened version, reconstructed the Capital One attack chain, and traced each new finding in a drifted configuration to the change that caused it. Compared on the same code, the open-source scanner Checkov detected 35 of the 40 issues and covered much more ground overall, but missed four real problems, one because a provider had renamed an attribute. The results show that layered controls and tested rules are needed, and that no single scanner is enough.

**Keywords:** cloud security, misconfiguration, shared responsibility model, infrastructure as code, policy as code, Terraform, CSPM, attack paths, configuration drift, AWS, Azure
